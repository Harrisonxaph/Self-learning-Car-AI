### 0.2 The decision follows the question (supersedes "Primary measure", "Why ordered progress" and "Secondary, descriptive, decide nothing", and decisions 3 and 4)

The question is unchanged: **does learned braking improve lap time and/or collisions on unseen circuits?** The deciding measures are the paired **collision** and **lap-time** analyses below; ordered progress is kept as an **additional, descriptive endpoint** and no longer decides. All rules are predeclared here with defaults for the owner and Codex to confirm or correct before the protocol is frozen; the numeric thresholds below are **proposed defaults, not yet owner-approved**.

- **Unit and evaluation.** The pair (seed) is the unit, as in E4. Each frozen champion is evaluated on each fresh circuit from the evaluator's **five fixed starts** (the nominal start and the four perturbed starts of P4-05), not one: a collision rate needs more than one scenario. The five starts are scenario variation of one deterministic champion, **not independent samples**; the intervals below are over the ten seeds only. If the gate cannot supply the five starts, extending it (under the same once-only and listed-agent rules) is part of the library rework, and is reviewed as such.
- **Collision endpoint (always defined).** Per champion and circuit, the crash rate over the five starts (0 to 1). Per pair, `c_s = crashRate(A5_s) − crashRate(A4_s)` (negative favours the brake). Mean over seeds, seed-cluster bootstrap with the E3 and E4 settings, predeclared meaningful difference **delta_c = 0.20** (one start in five), labels `a5-fewer`, `a4-fewer`, `no-clear-difference` by the same rule as before (interval entirely beyond zero and the mean at least delta_c).
- **Lap-time endpoint (survivor-conditioned, so conditioned on both completing).** Per pair and circuit, the mean first-lap time (simulated seconds) over the starts **where both champions completed a lap**; the pair is **eligible** only if it has at least one such start. `t_s = time(A5_s) − time(A4_s)` over the eligible pairs (negative favours the brake). **Minimum evidence for a lap-time decision: at least 6 of the 10 pairs eligible** (proposed default); with fewer, the lap-time endpoint is reported as **`inconclusive`** with the counts, never decided and never replaced by another measure. Over the eligible pairs: the mean, a seed-cluster bootstrap, predeclared meaningful difference **delta_t = 1.0 simulated second** (proposed default; to be confirmed against the development circuits before freezing, which is what they are for), labels `a5-faster`, `a4-faster`, `no-clear-difference`. Each agent's own number of completed laps, the starts and pairs excluded and the reason are shown; a missing time is never zero, never imputed and never dropped from one side only.
- **Survivor-bias guard (the harm check).** A lap-time advantage among survivors is not a benefit if the brake made the champion crash meaningfully more, and a collision advantage is not a benefit if it made laps meaningfully slower. So the per-circuit outcome is one of: **`a5-helps`** (at least one endpoint favours A5 under its label and neither endpoint favours A4), **`a4-better`** (the mirror image), **`mixed`** (one endpoint favours each), **`no-clear-difference`** (neither endpoint is clear) and, when lap time is `inconclusive` and collisions are `no-clear-difference`, **`inconclusive`**. A floor effect (all or nearly all champions crash on every start) is reported by its counts as the likeliest reason for `inconclusive` or `no-clear-difference` and is never read as "no effect".
- **Claims.** "Learned braking helps" may be stated only for a circuit whose outcome is `a5-helps`, for **these ten champion pairs on that circuit**, and never pooled across circuits (no pooled headline; if the circuits differ the report says so and stops). "No clear difference" and `inconclusive` do not mean equivalence or no effect. Two endpoints on several circuits are **not corrected for multiplicity**; the intervals are pointwise, stated in the report. The report also shows ordered progress (the E3 and E4 measure), crashes, lap completions and each champion's brake usage (a tick above a brake command of 0.05), all descriptive.
- **Why this is not post-hoc: two freezes, in this order.** (1) The **analysis-design freeze** is this section (the endpoints, rules, thresholds, minimum counts and the five starts), made **before any fresh circuit exists**; once this correction is reviewed and merged, changing any of it is a recorded, reviewed amendment made before the circuits are generated, never after. The three spent circuits may inform the numeric thresholds only before this freeze, and that use is recorded. (2) The **final manifest freeze** (step 5) happens **after** the fresh circuits are registered (their content hashes and the split-manifest hash cannot exist earlier, and the manifest parser verifies them) and **before** any evaluation on them; it adds the circuits and the frozen commit and may not alter the analysis design. Nothing is evaluated on a fresh circuit before freeze (2).

## 3. Design

**The question.** E4 found no direction on the training circuit. E5 asks whether the brake output makes a measurable difference on circuits the agents did not train on. It is a **paired** comparison: for each seed the two agents started from the identical generation-0 population and evolved separately, so the pair is the unit.

**Two designs (decision 1 for the owner).**
- **Design A (proposed default): evaluate E4's ten frozen A4 and A5 champions on the held-out circuits.** No new training. The champions come unchanged from the committed E4 records (checked by their frozen identity and the record hashes), so E5 is a **held-out evaluation of an already-reviewed, already-recorded population**: 20 champions on each held-out circuit, one nominal-start evaluation each (the agents are deterministic), so 60 evaluations for three circuits. A few minutes of compute and no training run.
- **Design B: new seeds, trained afresh** (a new reserved block, proposed 4001 to 4010, the E4 machinery at C1): about 10 seeds at roughly 4 minutes per seed per agent plus replays, so roughly one to two hours, and a new manifest, records and reserved-seed rules. More independent of E4, at a real cost, and it spends nothing the held-out look has not already spent.
- **Why A is the default:** it adds no new training, is fully checkable from committed data, and keeps E5 a pure held-out question. **Its cost, stated:** the pairs are E4's, so E5 is **not independent of E4's seeds** (it tests the same ten pairs on new circuits), and the champions were chosen by training-circuit fitness, which is the point of asking about transfer.

**[Superseded by section 0.2: progress no longer decides.] Primary measure.** Per held-out circuit, `d_s = progress(A5_s) - progress(A4_s)` with **ordered progress** (gates per lap) of each frozen champion, paired by seed, and the same meaningful threshold **delta = 0.25 lap**, a seed-cluster bootstrap with the E3 and E4 settings, and a new decision rule `p5-02-e5-decision-v1` with the E3 three labels (`a5-higher`, `a4-higher`, `no-clear-difference`) and the E3 wording. **No pooled headline across circuits** (E3's rule): if the circuits disagree, the report says so and stops.

**[Superseded by sections 0.1 and 0.2.] Why ordered progress, and an honest disclosure.** Lap time is missing for any agent that crashes, and E3 showed that nearly every agent crashes early on these circuits (A5's median progress was 0.04 to 1.0 laps), so lap time alone would be unavailable for most pairs. The choice of progress as the primary measure is therefore **informed by what E3 showed about these circuits**; it is stated here, before any E5 evaluation, and not chosen after seeing E5 results.

**[Superseded by section 0.2: collisions and lap time decide; the rest stays descriptive.] Secondary, descriptive, decide nothing:** collisions (crash counts per agent and circuit, which the roadmap asks for); lap completions; **first-lap time only over the pairs where both agents completed a lap**, with each agent's own sample count and the excluded pairs named (the rule P5-05 settled: a missing time is never zero and never dropped from one side only); the brake-usage of each champion on each circuit (the E4 threshold, a tick counts as braking above a brake command of 0.05) so the report can say whether A5 used the brake at all on unseen circuits.

**Intervals are pointwise per circuit, not a simultaneous guarantee** across the three circuits (Codex's note on E3); the report states this limit.

## 5. What is deliberately not claimed, and the limits

- **[Superseded by section 0.1: E5's held-out conclusion now rests on fresh circuits; this bullet records the original, rejected reasoning.]** **The held-out circuits have already been looked at once** (E3, for different agents). They are "unseen" for these agents only in the sense that no E5 agent was trained or tuned on them; E3's results were known when this plan was written, and that disclosure is part of the record. A second study on the same three circuits spends further of their unseenness; P5-04 would spend more.
- E5 supports a statement about **these ten champion pairs on these three circuits** and this metric. It does not support a statement about braking in general, other circuits, other configurations, or learning beyond them.
- A "no clear difference" label does **not** mean "no effect" or "equivalent". It is the likeliest result if both agents crash early on every circuit (a floor effect); that would be reported plainly as such.
- A1 to A3 baselines are not part of E5 (A3 and the rules were E3's; E6 is P5-04).
