## 1. What P4-07 must deliver (ROADMAP, issue #58)

- **Description:** a render-only camera with fit-whole-track, zoom and follow-a-car (default: the current best car) modes, so large circuits stay legible on phones. Silverstone is about 1.0 by 1.7 km, so a fitted 4 m car is about 1 px on a phone. The camera never changes simulation state, sensors or results.
- **Acceptance:** fit, zoom and follow work on desktop, phone and tablet. Zoom controls are keyboard accessible and at least 44 px. Pinch zoom affects the canvas only. **Camera changes leave training results identical for the same seed (test enforced).**
- **Tests:** unit for world to screen transforms and clamping; integration that results are unchanged by the camera; E2E fit, zoom and follow in Chromium and WebKit phone projects; manual real iPhone and iPad check.

## 3. Design

- **`src/render/camera.ts` (new, pure, headless, in `render`).** An immutable `Camera` (`mode: 'fit' | 'zoom' | 'follow'`, `zoom` as a multiple of the fit scale, a world-space `centre`) and pure functions that each return a new camera or the same one. **Every screen-space operation receives an explicit `CameraContext` (`{ bounds, cssWidth, cssHeight }`, the track's bounds and the canvas size in CSS pixels)**, because the fit scale depends on the canvas size: the same camera at two canvas sizes has two different scales, so a drag of a given number of pixels is a different number of metres. They are `fitCamera(bounds)`, `zoomBy(camera, factor, anchor, context)` (about a screen point, so a pinch or wheel keeps the world point under the fingers fixed: it converts the anchor to the world with `viewOf(camera, context)`, changes the zoom, and re-centres so that world point maps back to the same screen point), `panBy(camera, dx, dy, context)` (screen pixels, converted to metres with the current scale), `follow(camera, targetWorldPosition)` (world space; no conversion needed), and `setMode`. The context is also what `viewOf` takes, so a camera is never interpreted against a transform other than the one it is drawn with. Limits: zoom from 1 (the whole track) to a maximum that makes a 4 m car about 40 CSS px (a documented constant), clamped; the centre is clamped so the track's bounds never leave the view entirely. `viewOf(camera, bounds, size) => WorldToScreen` composes the fit view with the zoom and centre. It never throws and never reads simulation state: its inputs are numbers and a `Bounds`. At zoom 1 in fit mode `viewOf` returns **exactly** `fitBounds(...)`, so the default pages draw as they do today.
- **The track layer under a camera.** The cached layer stays as it is for the fit view (so the default look, the build counter and every current test are unchanged). When the camera is not at the fit view the page draws the track **directly** with `drawTrack` through the camera's view each frame instead of `drawImage` of the cached layer; `drawTrack` is the function the layer already uses, so the picture is the same. Silverstone's boundary is on the order of a thousand points, so a direct draw per frame is cheap on a phone; the plan measures it and keeps the cached layer if a frame budget is missed (a measured note in the PR, not a claim). Culling to the visible rectangle is **not** planned unless the measurement requires it.
- **Follow.** The follow target is chosen by a pure function of the **whole generation's** read-only snapshot, not of the drawn subset: on the training page, the **current best car** (the running car with the most ordered progress, ties to the lowest index; if none is running, the best finished car). **The followed car is always drawn.** The page's existing drawing cap (`effectiveDrawCap`, at most ten cars in Max mode) and `pickCars` select cars in population order, so the best car can lie outside the selected subset; the camera never centres on an invisible car, so the page reserves one slot **within the existing cap** for the followed car (it is added to the picked subset and the least-prioritised picked car is dropped when the cap would be exceeded, so the number of cars drawn never exceeds the cap). The cap, the picking rule for the other cars and the simulation are otherwise unchanged, and with the camera at fit and not following, `pickCars` is used exactly as today; on the driving page, the player's car; in a replay, the replayed car; in a comparison replay, a toggle between A and B. The camera smooths its centre towards the target (a documented fixed fraction per frame at 60 Hz, independent of the display rate), and the heading is **not** rotated (north up), so the track never spins. Following only reads positions the page already draws; it writes nothing.
- **Pinch, wheel and drag, on the canvas only.** Pointer Events: two pointers pinch (zoom about their midpoint) and pan; one pointer drags to pan in zoom mode (a drag in follow mode switches to zoom and pauses following, with a **Follow** button to resume); the mouse wheel zooms about the cursor. Each gesture calls the pure functions above. The race canvas already has `touch-action: none`, so nothing scrolls the page; the gestures are handled only on that canvas (a pinch elsewhere on the page is the browser's own), and driving controls keep their own handlers. A gesture ending, being cancelled or the pointer leaving never leaves the camera in an inconsistent state (tested).
- **Controls.** A small group under the canvas: **Fit**, **Zoom −**, **Zoom +**, **Follow** (a toggle for the mode's default target), and, on the comparison replay, **Follow A/B**. Each at least 44 CSS px, a text label and a tooltip, keyboard reachable in the page's tab order, `aria-pressed` for toggles, the current mode and zoom announced politely ("Zoom 4×, following the best car"). Keyboard shortcuts on the focused canvas: `+` and `-` zoom, `0` fits, `F` toggles follow; they do not clash with the driving keys (arrows, space, R), and they are inactive while a text field has focus.
- **Where the camera lives and what it may touch.** `render/camera.ts` is pure. The page code (`main.ts` for driving, `ui/trainingPage.ts` for training) owns one camera per page, feeds it the follow target and the gestures, and passes `viewOf(...)` to the existing draw functions. **`src/sim`, `src/sensors`, `src/ai`, `src/evolution`, `src/training` and `src/persistence` import nothing from `camera.ts`** (the lint boundaries already forbid the reverse direction for `render`, and a source scan adds that nothing in those folders names the camera). The trainer's `runGeneration` and the frame loop's simulation step receive no camera argument.
- **Published for the browser tests:** the camera's mode, zoom and centre on `canvas.dataset`, next to the existing transform data. At fit the existing `trackScale` and offsets are exactly as before.
- **No change to the formats, the saved models, the studies or the simulation.** Nothing is stored (the camera resets on reload; decision 6).

## 4. What is deliberately not claimed

The camera shows what the simulation already computed, larger. It does not make a model drive better, make a circuit easier, or change any result, and no claim about learning or generalisation rests on it. A followed best car is a display choice, not a ranking of models. Real iPhone and iPad behaviour (pinch feel, gesture conflicts with browser chrome, frame rate while zoomed) is established only by the owner's device checks, never inferred from the emulated CI projects.

## 5. Decisions for the owner (defaults proposed; each routine unless changed)

1. **Render-only camera in `src/render/camera.ts`**, pure and headless, never imported by the simulation, training or persistence code.
2. **Modes fit, zoom and follow;** the default is fit, so every page looks as it does today until the person zooms or follows.
3. **Follow the current best car** (most ordered progress among running cars, ties to the lowest index) on the training page; the player's car when driving; the replayed car in a replay; an A/B toggle in the comparison replay. **North up** (the view is never rotated).
4. **Zoom range** from 1 (whole track) to the scale at which a 4 m car is about 40 CSS px; the centre is clamped so the track never leaves the view entirely.
5. **Gestures on the race canvas only:** two-pointer pinch and pan, one-pointer drag to pan in zoom mode (which pauses following), mouse wheel about the cursor. Pinch never zooms the page.
6. **The camera is not stored:** it resets to fit on reload and on a track or circuit change.
7. **Draw the track directly through the camera's view when not at the fit view;** keep the cached layer for the fit view, with a measured frame-time note and culling only if the measurement requires it.
8. **Controls:** Fit, Zoom −, Zoom +, Follow (and Follow A/B in the comparison replay), at least 44 px, with keyboard shortcuts `+`, `-`, `0` and `F` on the focused canvas.

## 6. Tests

- **Unit (`camera`):** `viewOf` at fit equals `fitBounds` exactly (several canvas sizes and aspect ratios, including a canvas too small to hold anything); `zoomBy` about an anchor keeps the world point under the anchor fixed (within rounding) at several zooms; zoom clamps at 1 and at the maximum; `panBy` clamps so the bounds never leave the view; `follow` smooths towards the target at a rate independent of the frame rate and settles on it; mode transitions (zoom to fit, follow to zoom on a drag, resume); every function is deterministic, never throws on non-finite or extreme input (NaN, infinities, huge zoom factors, zero-size bounds) and returns a valid camera; the best-car chooser (ties, no running cars, an empty population); **the screen-space operations at two different canvas sizes** (the same drag in pixels gives different world movement, and a zoom about an anchor keeps the anchored world point fixed at both sizes and in portrait and landscape); **the followed car and the drawing cap:** with a population larger than the cap and the best car outside the initially picked subset, the best car is drawn, the number of cars drawn does not exceed the cap (including Max mode's ten), and with no follow the picked subset is exactly today's.
- **Integration (the camera cannot change results, test enforced):** the same seeded training run (several generations, a few hundred ticks each) is executed with **no camera, a fitted camera, a zoomed camera, a following camera and a camera changed between frames**, driving the real page wiring in Node with fake frames and a recording canvas, and the full generation records, the champion's parameters and the next generation's population are **bit-identical** across all of them; the same for a manual-driving world stepped with scripted inputs and a replay. **Source scans** show that `sim`, `sensors`, `ai`, `evolution`, `training` and `persistence` never name the camera, and that the trainer and the simulation step take no camera argument. **A mutation check** (making the page write the camera's centre into a simulation input) fails these tests.
- **Rendering:** with a recording context, at fit the draw calls match the current ones (the cached layer is still used and `trackLayerBuilds` does not grow); zoomed, the track is drawn directly through the camera's view; cars, rays and the locator ring are drawn through the same view; a frame is drawn for every camera state without throwing.
- **E2E (Chromium and WebKit, desktop, phone and tablet):** fit is the default and the published transform is unchanged; the Zoom + and Zoom − buttons change the published zoom and the drawn scale; Fit returns to the default; Follow keeps the best car near the middle of the view as it moves (training) and the player's car (driving); a drag pauses following and Follow resumes it; a **two-finger pinch** (touch events dispatched through the browser's input emulation in the touch projects) zooms the canvas and **does not scroll or zoom the page**; the wheel zooms about the cursor on desktop; every control is at least 44 px and reachable by keyboard, the shortcuts work on the focused canvas and do not fire while a text field has focus, and driving by keyboard is unaffected; no horizontal scroll at 320 px; the comparison replay's A/B toggle; results of a seeded training run read from the page are the same with and without zooming (a short run).
- **Not tested here:** a physical device, real pinch feel, and the frame rate on a phone.

## 7. Owner checks (iPhone and iPad, before a completion request)

1. In `?mode=train`, start training, pinch to zoom the canvas (the page must not zoom or scroll), drag to pan, tap Fit and then Follow: the best car stays in view as it moves, in portrait and landscape.
2. On the driving page, zoom in and drive with the on-screen controls and keyboard: the controls still work and the car stays followed; the buttons are easy to tap (44 px).

## 8. Limits

The camera is display only: one canvas, north up, no minimap, and it resets on reload. A very large zoom on a phone redraws the track each frame (measured, see decision 7). Results are unchanged by construction and by test, not by assumption. ST1 to ST5, the three-split boundaries, the study held-out gate and ledger, every existing record and the E3 protocol are untouched.
