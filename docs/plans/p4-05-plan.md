# P4-05 plan: evaluate trained models on unfamiliar tracks

> **Status: plan only, for Codex review and the owner's merge. Implementation needs its own owner authorisation.**
> - **Authorisation:** the owner's "Continue development" (2026-11-12, after #165 merged), read as the next Phase 4 step. P4-03 needs P4-05, P4-05 needs P4-04 (merged) and P3-05 (Complete), and P4-06 has its own merged plan but no implementation authorisation. As for earlier items it is read as authorising a **plan only**.
> - **Scope:** the roadmap entry and issue #32: an evaluation runner and UI that runs a saved model on chosen built-in circuits with fixed evaluation seeds and stores the results in the model's `evaluation` field, showing lap completion, collisions and lap time. **Not** in scope: any study (E3, E5, E6, P5-04, P5-05 come from preregistered protocols and the command line, never from this page), custom circuits (P4-03 plugs into the interface defined here), training, any physics change, any learning claim.
> - **Lessons carried from P2-10, P4-04 and the E3 work:** the held-out boundary is the integrity of every generalisation claim, so a page that can show held-out results needs rules that make a breach visible; a deterministic episode repeated is one trial, not many; results are labelled by what they are.

## 1. What P4-05 must deliver (ROADMAP, issue #32)

- **Description:** an evaluation runner and UI that runs a saved model on chosen built-in tracks with fixed evaluation seeds and stores results in the model's `evaluation` field.
- **Acceptance:** **Phase 4 acceptance (part): users can evaluate trained models on built-in layouts they did not train on and see lap completion, collisions and lap time.** Custom layouts are P4-03.
- **Tests:** integration: evaluation deterministic. E2E: evaluate a saved model on a built-in held-out track.
- **MODEL_FORMAT:** each real evaluation entry includes protocol and configuration version, track ID, version and split, frozen model ID, evaluation seeds and starts, episode and outcome counts, metric definitions and summary statistics; absent completed laps give a null lap time with a zero sample count; no traces are embedded.

## 3. Design

- **`src/training/modelEvaluation.ts` (new, pure, headless).** `evaluateModel(model, circuits, options)` evaluates one saved model on a list of circuits under one fixed protocol and returns a plain result. It builds the driver from the model's own recorded `brakeEnabled`, physics and sensors (an A4-type model is evaluated with its brake masked, as trained), runs each episode through `evaluateDriver`, and **never reads a track directly**: circuits arrive as `EvaluationTarget`s (`{ id, version, split, track }`) from the caller, which is how P4-03 later adds saved custom circuits (split `unregistered`) with no change here. Circuit **IDs** are chosen by `src/ui/evaluatePage.ts` and the tests; `modelEvaluation.ts` resolves them (section 3b) and no module receives a `Track` from the page.
- **The protocol, `p4-05-evaluation-v1`, fixed in code and recorded in every entry.**
  - Limits and scoring: `DEFAULT_EVALUATION_CONFIG` (60 s, 5 s stall, 3 laps, fitness version 1), recorded with a configuration hash.
  - **Starts and evaluation seeds.** A model is deterministic, so the same start repeated is **one** trial, not many (AI_EVALUATION §5). The protocol therefore uses the circuit's nominal start (seed 0) plus **four recorded perturbed starts** (the fixed seed list `[1, 2, 3, 4]`): each is a small offset from the nominal start drawn from the repository's seeded RNG (lateral at most 1.0 m, heading at most 3 degrees), checked to start on the road without touching a wall. The offsets, not just the seeds, are recorded. These starts are scenario variation of one model on one circuit; they are **not** independent training runs and give no uncertainty about training (decision 2 offers the simpler nominal-only protocol).
  - Per circuit the entry holds the **raw rows** (one small row per start: outcome, ordered progress, gates, laps, first-lap time in simulation ticks and seconds, or null) and the **summary**: lap completion (episodes with at least one full lap ÷ episodes), collision rate (episodes ending in a wall collision ÷ episodes), the count of each terminal outcome, mean ordered progress, and mean first-lap time **with its sample count**, `null` and `0` when no lap completed (never zero). Timeouts and stalls stay in every denominator.
- **The entry format and strict validation.** `evaluation` entries get `kind: 'p4-05-evaluation-v1'` with: protocol and configuration version and hash, the model's **frozen identity** (`freezeModel(parameters, provenance)`'s ID), the circuit's ID, version, split and content hash, the seed list and start offsets, the per-start rows and the summary, an `exploratory` flag and, for held-out circuits, `heldOutLook: true`, a `recordedAt` time, the app and code versions, and a `runtime` note ("browser; results can differ slightly between browsers and engines (Math.sin and Math.cos), as AI_EVALUATION §4 says"). `modelFormat` validates **entries of this kind strictly** (finite numbers, counts that add up, the summary recomputed from the rows, the circuit's recorded hash checked against the registry) and keeps accepting every older document: an empty or missing `evaluation` is valid as before, and an entry of an unknown kind is kept opaque and untouched. **No new count or per-entry rule is applied to documents that already exist:** the field has only ever been checked to be an array, so a model that already carries more than 50 entries (opaque ones from another tool, or of any kind) must still import, load, replay and export **byte for byte**, as it does today; the existing whole-document size limit is the only bound on import. The limit of **50 entries applies only to `appendEvaluation`, counting this app's own `p4-05-evaluation-v1` entries**; a model at or over it simply cannot take another one (the page says why) and is otherwise untouched. The format's schema version is unchanged (the field already exists).
- **Held-out circuits in the browser (the main integrity decision), revised after review.** A frozen model hash proves *which model* was looked at; it does **not** stop the person who looks from using what they saw to shape a later preregistered study, so a hash is not an integrity rule. The plan therefore protects the **circuits**, not the models:
  1. **Deny by default, per circuit.** A held-out circuit is offered in the browser only if it appears in an **append-only registry of exploratory circuits** (`EXPLORATORY_HELD_OUT`, in `trackSplits.ts`, empty until a PR adds to it). **Every circuit that any preregistered protocol lists, and every held-out circuit by default, is study-reserved and is never offered, evaluated or displayed by this page.** At the time of this plan that is all three built-in held-out circuits: the E3 manifest (merged, #165) lists `hairpin-chicane`, `long-sweepers` and `technical-narrow`. Study-reserved circuits stay reachable only through the command line, with its manifest and ledger.
  2. **The two sets can never overlap, and a study cannot absorb an exploratory circuit.** A test-enforced rule: no study manifest parser (P2-08, E4, E3, and any later kind) accepts a circuit that is in `EXPLORATORY_HELD_OUT`, and no circuit is added to `EXPLORATORY_HELD_OUT` if any merged manifest lists it. A circuit's designation is never changed after any result about it exists (AI_EVALUATION §5: such a circuit becomes a validation circuit and a new held-out circuit is made). Results from an exploratory circuit are labelled exploratory everywhere and **never enter a study record, an analysis or a claim**.
  3. **To keep the roadmap's held-out E2E honest, the plan proposes one new circuit**, a fourth held-out-split circuit generated by the same deterministic generator, specified by criteria before anything is evaluated, **designated exploratory-only from birth** (its provenance says so; it is never listed by any manifest). It is added by its own epoch (epoch 3 of the append-only registry; the three study-reserved circuits and every earlier record are unchanged) in the implementation PR, with the owner's approval of its criteria in this plan's decision 1. Without it, the browser offers validation circuits and other training circuits only.
  4. The exploratory circuit is reached **only** through `heldOutTrack(id, frozenModel)` (frozen models only), and the page labels it exploratory: "an exploratory look, not a study result". The held-out rows need an explicit confirmation tick before Run. **No exploratory-held-out figure appears anywhere else:** not on the training dashboard or graph, not in the saved-champions list, not in the comparison view (which never ranks by evaluation results), not in automatic champion selection.
  5. **One look per frozen model and exploratory circuit, with a persistent, guarded record (section 3a).** The look is the *revealed result*.
  6. This page's looks are **not** the preregistered E3, E5 or E6 openings: those happen only through the command line, on frozen study agents, on study-reserved circuits.
- **3a. Guarded evaluation and the persistent one-look record (new, after review).** The rule is **"show only what is stored"**: an exploratory-held-out result is shown to the user **only after** it has been durably stored, so nothing is revealed that is not also counted, and the look is exactly the revealed result.
  - **Cancellation.** There is no partial result for an exploratory circuit: the five episodes run to completion in memory and nothing is displayed or stored until they finish. **Cancel before the store write** (or the tab closing, a crash or a navigation) reveals nothing and stores nothing, so the look is **not** used and may be taken again; the evaluation is deterministic, so a retry gives the identical result. Validation and other-training circuits may show progress as it comes and store each finished circuit.
  - **Storage failures.** The store write is all-or-nothing (the model document is replaced in one `setItem`, like a save). If storage is full, disabled or the write throws, the page shows the same messages as the saved-champions panel and **shows no result** (not "unsaved results"): the look is not used. If the model write succeeds and the second write fails (below), the result is shown and the failure is reported with a retry that repairs it on the next load.
  - **Two records.** The authoritative record is the entry in the model's `evaluation` field. A second, **separate look ledger** in browser storage (`sdae:eval-looks`: frozen model ID and circuit ID, never deleted when a model is deleted or re-imported) is written after the model entry, so deleting a model, importing a stripped copy of it, or exporting and re-importing does **not** reset a look in the same browser; on every load a model entry with `heldOutLook` that is missing from the ledger is added to it. The ledger is a **guard, not security**: clearing site data resets it.
  - **Concurrent tabs and windows.** The check-then-write runs inside one critical section: the Web Locks API (`navigator.locks.request`, available in current Safari, Chrome and Firefox) around *read ledger and model, refuse if a look exists, write model, write ledger*. Where `navigator.locks` is absent the page uses a lock key with a unique token written, re-read and verified before and after the critical section, and says in the page note that two tabs racing in the same instant may both compute (never both reveal different results: the evaluation is deterministic and a second writer **re-reads and shows the stored entry**, not its own). A tab that loses the race, or finds a look already stored, **shows the stored result and runs nothing further**. A `storage` event refreshes every open evaluate page, so a second tab sees the look as taken.
  - **What stays out of reach:** nothing here can stop devtools reading a result in memory before the write, or a user clearing storage. The plan says so on the page and in ARCHITECTURE; the labels and the ledger make an accidental or casual repeat visible, they do not make a determined one impossible.
- **3b. Follow-up after the formal review of #166 (this section supersedes any earlier wording it contradicts).** The first formal review of this plan asked for four things the merged text only partly settled; they are fixed here, before any implementation is authorised.
  - **Exploratory results can influence studies unless the people designing studies stay independent of them; a different model hash does not protect a study.** Anything learned from an exploratory look (a reward weight, a hyperparameter, an architecture, a selection rule, a circuit design) is contamination of a later generalisation claim, whoever's model it was. So: (1) **study design and selection must remain independent of exploratory results**; every future study protocol (E5, E6, P5-04) carries an explicit line stating which exploratory circuits and looks existed when it was written and that none influenced it. (2) **Exposure is recorded:** each exploratory look is an entry in the model's `evaluation` field (circuit, time, model, app and code version) and, for looks made on the owner's or developers' own models during development, a line in the PR or issue that caused them; manual looks and E2E runs are separate from the protected study fixtures (below). (3) **If an exploratory result drives any change** to how models are trained, chosen or evaluated, the exploratory circuit is **reclassified as a validation circuit and a fresh exploratory circuit is made** before anything is claimed about generalisation (AI_EVALUATION §5); the "for that model" wording in the page's notice is replaced by "for any later work that you or anyone else does with this result". (4) **CI and tests never evaluate trained models on a study-reserved circuit**: the E2E and integration tests evaluate on validation and other training circuits, and, if the owner approves decision 1A, on the exploratory circuit only; a source scan fails any test that passes a study-reserved ID to the browser evaluation module.
  - **The browser held-out execution contract: one narrow entry point, no bypass.** `createEvaluationSetup` and `assertEvaluationTrack` keep rejecting every held-out circuit, and `buildEvaluationSetup` stays referenced only by `evaluation.ts` and `heldOutAccess.ts` (test-enforced). The exploratory path is a **new function inside `heldOutAccess.ts`'s sibling module `exploratoryAccess.ts`, which is the only place that may resolve an exploratory circuit**: it takes a **circuit ID, never a `Track`**, looks it up in `EXPLORATORY_HELD_OUT` (refusing every other ID, as a renamed copy and by geometry), requires a **frozen model** (`heldOutTrack`'s rule), and builds its episodes through the same `buildEvaluationSetup` path as the studies with the model's own recorded physics and sensors, the split label `held-out` and a start **override pose** (a parameter of the episode, not a change to the track). The track object, its ID and its content hash are **never altered, relabelled or copied** to pass a split check: the recorded entry carries the circuit's real ID, version and hash, and a test shows a perturbed start leaves `track` deep-equal to the registered one. **Ownership of target selection:** the page chooses *circuit IDs* (from the three labelled groups) and nothing else; `modelEvaluation.ts` resolves training and validation IDs through `trainingTrack` and `validationTrack` and exploratory IDs only through `exploratoryAccess.ts`; no module receives a `Track` from the page. The source-scan allowlist names `exploratoryAccess.ts` (for `heldOutTrack` and the held-out split) and `modelEvaluation.ts` (for `validationTrack` and `trainingTrack`), nothing else.
  - **A persistent claim before inference, failing closed (this replaces "show only what is stored" as the sole rule; both now hold).** For an exploratory circuit the sequence is: acquire the critical section (Web Locks, or the verified-token fallback) → re-read the model entry and the look ledger → refuse if a look exists in **any** state → **write a claim** (`state: 'claimed'`, claim ID, time) to the model entry **and** the ledger → release the lock → run the episodes → write the result over the claim (`state: 'completed'`) → show it. **If the claim cannot be recorded (storage full, disabled, throwing, a lock that cannot be acquired) nothing runs:** the page fails closed with the storage messages and no look is used. **A claim with no result is a consumed look:** a cancel after the claim, a crash, a closed tab or a failed result write leaves `state: 'claimed'` (reported on the next load as "interrupted: this look was started but no result was stored and it counts as taken"); **no result for it is ever shown or stored later**, and it is never silently re-run. A completed entry is returned from storage on every later request and runs nothing. The held-out run is not cancellable once the claim is written (it takes a fraction of a second; cancellation before the claim stores nothing and uses nothing). **Concurrent tabs cannot both acquire a look:** only the tab that wrote the claim inside the critical section computes; every other tab finds the claim and shows "a look is in progress or completed in another tab" and the stored result when it appears (a `storage` event refreshes it). A deliberately cleared or edited storage still resets the guard, as stated in the limits.
  - **Older models with more than 50 entries: a backward-compatible rule with explicit tests.** Importing, loading, replaying and exporting a model never truncates, rejects or rewrites its `evaluation` array; the only bound on import is the existing whole-document size limit. The cap of 50 applies **only when appending this app's own entries**, counts only those, and a model at or over it **refuses the append with a message and is otherwise untouched**. Tests: a pre-change fixture (written by the unchanged code) with **51** opaque entries and others with 120 and with 60 of this app's own kind: each imports, round-trips byte for byte through import and export, and an append to each is refused with the cap message and leaves the stored document byte-identical.

- **The page: `?mode=evaluate` (new page mode).** The default page, `?mode=train` and `?mode=edit` are unchanged. A model list (from the model store; a damaged or non-drivable model is shown with its reason and cannot be run), then circuit checkboxes in three labelled groups: **Other training circuits**, **Validation circuits**, **Held-out circuits (exploratory)**, with the circuit the model was trained on marked and not offered as "unfamiliar" (it may be ticked, and is labelled "training circuit"). **Run** evaluates the chosen circuits in short slices (`setTimeout` yields, so the page stays responsive on a phone), with a progress line and a **Cancel** (for validation and training circuits it keeps the finished circuits; for an exploratory-held-out circuit it reveals and stores nothing, section 3a); each finished circuit is stored in the model's `evaluation` field through a new `appendEvaluation` store operation that validates the entry and writes it the way a save does (an all-or-nothing write, with the storage-full and disabled-storage messages the saved-champions panel already has), inside the guarded critical section of 3a for exploratory-held-out circuits. The results table shows, per circuit and split: lap completion, collision rate, outcome counts, mean first-lap time with n, mean ordered progress, and a note that a deterministic model on five starts is one model's behaviour, not a statistic. All controls are at least 44 px and keyboard operable; the table is a real `<table>` with a caption and the status line is a polite live region; nothing scrolls sideways at 320 px.
- **Determinism and replay.** Evaluation is a pure function of the model, the circuit's recorded hash and the protocol, in one engine: running it twice gives identical entries (apart from `recordedAt`), and a test shows that **the stored entry replays**: re-running the protocol on the recorded model, circuit and seed list reproduces every row. Cross-browser differences are recorded, not hidden.
- **No change to the trainer, the split code or the formats beyond the `evaluation` entry kind.** The three-split boundaries and the study held-out gate are untouched; the two new modules named in section 3b are added to the **source-scan allowlist** deliberately and nothing else is, so a later module reaching for the restricted accessors still fails a test.

## 4. What is deliberately not claimed

Evaluating one model on a few circuits from a few starts shows how that model drove there; it does not show that evolution generalises, that braking helps, or that any rule or baseline is beaten: those are studies (P5-04, E3, E5, E6) with preregistered protocols, ten training seeds and the command line. Perturbed starts are scenario variation, not independent trials. Lap time is simulation time. Held-out circuits viewed here are "unseen" only until the user looks. No learning claim is made.

## 5. Decisions for the owner (defaults proposed; each routine unless changed)

1. **Held-out circuits in the browser: deny by default, per circuit** (section 3). All three existing held-out circuits are **study-reserved** (the E3 manifest lists them) and are **never** offered by the browser. To keep the roadmap's held-out E2E, **add one new exploratory-only held-out-split circuit** (recommended; its own epoch, generated, criteria written before anything is evaluated, never listed by any manifest, specified in the implementation PR for the owner's approval). Alternatives: **(B)** offer validation and other training circuits only and **drop the held-out E2E** (changes the roadmap's test line; the owner's say), or **(C)** offer a study-reserved circuit as an exploratory look anyway (**not recommended**: it lets what the user sees shape later studies, which a model hash cannot prevent).
2. **Start protocol:** the nominal start plus four seeded perturbed starts (recommended, so five episodes per circuit show some sensitivity), or the nominal start only (one episode per circuit, simplest, no start variation).
3. **A new page mode `?mode=evaluate`** (recommended) rather than a panel inside the training page, which is already crowded on a phone.
4. **The entry format** `p4-05-evaluation-v1`, strictly validated for its own entries only, with unknown kinds kept opaque and **no new count or per-entry rule applied to existing documents** (a model with more than 50 entries still imports, loads and exports byte for byte); the model schema version is unchanged.
5. **Evaluation results never feed the dashboard, the comparison view or automatic selection**, and are never ranked.
6. **At most 50 of this app's own `p4-05-evaluation-v1` entries per model, enforced only when appending** (never on import), stored in the model's `evaluation` field in browser storage with the existing safety rules.
8. **"Show only what is stored" for exploratory-held-out results, with a persistent look ledger** and a critical section across tabs (Web Locks, with a verified-token fallback), cancellation and storage failures that reveal nothing and use no look (section 3a). The ledger is a guard, not security.
9. **A study cannot list an exploratory circuit and an exploratory circuit cannot be one a manifest lists**, test-enforced in every study manifest parser, so exploratory results can never contaminate a preregistered study.
10. **Persistent claim before inference, failing closed** (section 3b): a claimed look with no stored result is a consumed look and is never re-run or shown; the held-out run is not cancellable once claimed.
11. **Independence and exposure:** every later study protocol states which exploratory looks existed and that none influenced it; an exploratory result that drives a change reclassifies the circuit as validation and a fresh exploratory circuit is made; CI never evaluates trained models on a study-reserved circuit.
7. **The two new modules on the source-scan allowlist** (section 3b), taking circuit IDs and never tracks.

## 6. Tests

- **Unit (`modelEvaluation`):** the protocol's constants; the start perturbations are deterministic for a seed, within the stated bounds and always on the road without a wall touch (checked on **every** built-in circuit and a sweep of seeds); the driver is built from the model's own brake flag (an A4-type model really has its brake masked); the summary equals a hand recomputation from the rows on fixed fixtures (lap completion, collision rate, outcome counts, mean first-lap time with n, `null` and `0` with no completed lap); timeouts and stalls stay in the denominators; hostile models (wrong parameter count, non-finite values, an unfrozen copy) are refused with messages, never a raw exception.
- **Integration (determinism and replay):** evaluating the same saved model twice gives identical entries but for `recordedAt`; a stored entry replays row for row; a tampered row, count, hash or seed list is refused by the validator; every older model file (including the committed fixtures) still imports and its empty `evaluation` is valid.
- **Held-out rules (circuits, not hashes):** **every study-reserved held-out circuit is refused** by the browser evaluation (by ID, as a renamed copy and by geometry), including when the model is frozen and valid, and **no figure for one is ever produced or displayed**; the exploratory circuit (if approved) is reached only through `heldOutTrack` with a frozen model, and an unfrozen or edited model is refused; the confirmation is required; no exploratory figure reaches the dashboard, the saved list or the comparison view (a source scan and a rendering check); **the two sets never overlap**: every study manifest parser (P2-08, E4, E3) refuses an exploratory circuit and the registry refuses a circuit any merged manifest lists; the module appears on the allowlist and nothing else does.
- **One look, guarded (section 3a), on fakes with an interleaving scheduler:** cancel before the write reveals nothing and stores nothing and the look can be retaken with an identical result; a storage-full, disabled or throwing write shows **no** result and uses no look; a second write failure (ledger) shows the result and is repaired on the next load; **two tabs** over one fake storage, with the critical section both present (a fake `navigator.locks`) and absent (the token fallback), racing at every interleaving point: exactly one stored entry, both tabs show the stored entry, never two different results; a deleted model, a stripped re-import and an export and re-import in the same browser do not reset the look (the ledger); a model entry missing from the ledger is added on load; a `storage` event refreshes the other tab.
- **Store and compatibility:** `appendEvaluation` is all-or-nothing, counts only this app's own entries toward the cap of 50 **and only when appending**, respects the size limit, reports storage-full and disabled-storage and leaves other models untouched; **a model that already has 120 opaque entries, and one with 60 entries of this app's kind, still imports, loads, replays and exports byte for byte** (the committed fixtures plus new ones written by the unchanged code before the format change); an old model that is over the cap cannot take another entry and says why, and is otherwise untouched.
- **Page wiring (Node, fakes) and E2E (Chromium and WebKit, desktop, phone and tablet):** save a champion, open `?mode=evaluate`, evaluate it on the **other training circuit** and a **validation circuit**, see the table, reload and see the stored result; evaluate on a **held-out circuit** (the roadmap's E2E) with the confirmation and see the exploratory label, and see that asking again returns the recorded result without re-running; cancel stores nothing; a damaged model cannot be run; 44 px controls and no horizontal scroll at 320 px.
- **Mutation checks** during implementation: weakening the one-look rule, the frozen-model requirement, the summary denominators, the start bounds and the entry validation each fails a test.
- Not tested here: a physical device, and any statistical claim.

## 7. Owner checks (iPhone and iPad, before a completion request)

1. Save a champion in `?mode=train`, open `?mode=evaluate`, pick it, tick one training and one validation circuit and run: the progress line moves, the table appears, nothing scrolls sideways and the page stays responsive in portrait and landscape.
2. Tick a held-out circuit: the exploratory warning and confirmation are clear; run it; ask again and see the recorded result.
3. Reload: the results are still there; the controls are easy to tap (44 px).

## 8. Limits

One saved model, a handful of circuits and five starts: behaviour, not evidence. A browser cannot enforce the one-look rule against someone who clears storage or edits a file; the rule and the labels make a breach visible. Results can differ between browsers and engines. P4-03 adds saved custom circuits as targets of the same protocol; P4-07 adds camera work that this page does not need. ST1 to ST5, the held-out gate and ledger used by the studies, every existing record and the E3 protocol are untouched.
