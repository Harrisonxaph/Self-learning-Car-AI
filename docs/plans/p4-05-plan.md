# P4-05 plan: evaluate trained models on unfamiliar tracks

> **Status: plan only, for Codex review and the owner's merge. Implementation needs its own owner authorisation.**
> - **Authorisation:** the owner's "Continue development" (2026-11-12, after #165 merged), read as the next Phase 4 step. P4-03 needs P4-05, P4-05 needs P4-04 (merged) and P3-05 (Complete), and P4-06 has its own merged plan but no implementation authorisation. As for earlier items it is read as authorising a **plan only**.
> - **Scope:** the roadmap entry and issue #32: an evaluation runner and UI that runs a saved model on chosen built-in circuits with fixed evaluation seeds and stores the results in the model's `evaluation` field, showing lap completion, collisions and lap time. **Not** in scope: any study (E3, E5, E6, P5-04, P5-05 come from preregistered protocols and the command line, never from this page), custom circuits (P4-03 plugs into the interface defined here), training, any physics change, any learning claim.
> - **Lessons carried from P2-10, P4-04 and the E3 work:** the held-out boundary is the integrity of every generalisation claim, and a frozen-model hash cannot stop what a person sees from shaping a later study, so this page never touches held-out circuits at all (decided with the owner, below); a deterministic episode repeated is one trial, not many; results are labelled by what they are.
> - **Owner decisions recorded here (2026-11-12):** asked whether to drop the roadmap's held-out E2E or add a new exploratory-only held-out circuit, and given a recommendation to drop it, the owner replied **"Let's do it then"**: the browser offers no held-out circuit, and the roadmap's P4-05 test line changes from "evaluate a saved model on a built-in held-out track" to "on a built-in validation track and on another training track".

## 1. What P4-05 must deliver (ROADMAP, issue #32)

- **Description:** an evaluation runner and UI that runs a saved model on chosen built-in tracks with fixed evaluation seeds and stores results in the model's `evaluation` field.
- **Acceptance:** **Phase 4 acceptance (part): users can evaluate trained models on built-in layouts they did not train on and see lap completion, collisions and lap time.** Custom layouts are P4-03.
- **Tests:** integration: evaluation deterministic. E2E: evaluate a saved model on a built-in validation track and on another training track (**the roadmap's original line named a held-out track; changed on the owner's decision, above**).
- **MODEL_FORMAT:** each real evaluation entry includes protocol and configuration version, track ID, version and split, frozen model ID, evaluation seeds and starts, episode and outcome counts, metric definitions and summary statistics; absent completed laps give a null lap time with a zero sample count; no traces are embedded.

## 3. Design

- **`src/training/modelEvaluation.ts` (new, pure, headless).** `evaluateModel(model, circuits, options)` evaluates one saved model on a list of circuits under one fixed protocol and returns a plain result. It builds the driver from the model's own recorded `brakeEnabled`, physics and sensors (an A4-type model is evaluated with its brake masked, as trained), runs each episode through `evaluateDriver`, and takes **circuit IDs, never a `Track`**: it resolves each ID itself through `trainingTrack` or `validationTrack` (so a held-out ID is refused by the existing `TrackLeakageError`), which keeps one place responsible for which circuits may be evaluated. P4-03 later adds saved custom circuits (split `unregistered`) as a third source of targets behind the same function, with no change to the protocol. Circuit IDs are chosen by `src/ui/evaluatePage.ts` and the tests.
- **The protocol, `p4-05-evaluation-v1`, fixed in code and recorded in every entry.**
  - Limits and scoring: `DEFAULT_EVALUATION_CONFIG` (60 s, 5 s stall, 3 laps, fitness version 1), recorded with a configuration hash.
  - **Starts and evaluation seeds.** A model is deterministic, so the same start repeated is **one** trial, not many (AI_EVALUATION §5). The protocol therefore uses the circuit's nominal start (seed 0) plus **four recorded perturbed starts** (the fixed seed list `[1, 2, 3, 4]`): each is a small offset from the nominal start drawn from the repository's seeded RNG (lateral at most 1.0 m, heading at most 3 degrees), checked to start on the road without touching a wall. The offsets, not just the seeds, are recorded. These starts are scenario variation of one model on one circuit; they are **not** independent training runs and give no uncertainty about training (decision 2 offers the simpler nominal-only protocol).
  - Per circuit the entry holds the **raw rows** (one small row per start: outcome, ordered progress, gates, laps, first-lap time in simulation ticks and seconds, or null) and the **summary**: lap completion (episodes with at least one full lap ÷ episodes), collision rate (episodes ending in a wall collision ÷ episodes), the count of each terminal outcome, mean ordered progress, and mean first-lap time **with its sample count**, `null` and `0` when no lap completed (never zero). Timeouts and stalls stay in every denominator.
- **The entry format and strict validation.** `evaluation` entries get `kind: 'p4-05-evaluation-v1'` with: protocol and configuration version and hash, the model's **frozen identity** (`freezeModel(parameters, provenance)`'s ID), the circuit's ID, version, split (`training` or `validation`) and content hash, the seed list and start offsets, the per-start rows and the summary, a `recordedAt` time, the app and code versions, and a `runtime` note ("browser; results can differ slightly between browsers and engines (Math.sin and Math.cos), as AI_EVALUATION §4 says"). `modelFormat` validates **entries of this kind strictly** (finite numbers, counts that add up, the summary recomputed from the rows, a split that is not `held-out`, the circuit's recorded hash checked against the registry) and keeps accepting every older document: an empty or missing `evaluation` is valid as before, and an entry of an unknown kind is kept opaque and untouched. The format's schema version is unchanged (the field already exists). Backward compatibility for long arrays is in the next bullets.
- **No held-out circuit in the browser (decided with the owner, 2026-11-12).** The page offers **other training circuits** and **validation circuits** only. **Every held-out circuit is study-reserved** (the merged E3 manifest lists all three) and is **never offered, evaluated, resolved or displayed** by the browser, by any test, or by CI: held-out evaluation exists only through the preregistered command-line studies (E3, E5, E6, P5-04), with their manifests, frozen agents and ledgers. There is therefore no browser look to guard, no exploratory circuit, no look ledger and no browser lock. The reasons are in section 5, decision 1: a frozen-model hash proves which model was looked at but cannot stop what a person sees from shaping a later study, so the plan removes the path instead of guarding it. **Enforcement (test-enforced):** `modelEvaluation.ts` resolves circuit IDs only through `trainingTrack` and `validationTrack`, so a held-out ID is refused by the existing `TrackLeakageError` (by ID, as a renamed copy, and by geometry); it never imports `heldOutTrack` or `buildEvaluationSetup`; the page's circuit groups are built from `tracksIn('training')` and `tracksIn('validation')` only; and the source-scan allowlist (P4-04) lists `modelEvaluation.ts` for `trainingTrack` and `validationTrack` and **no new module** for `heldOutTrack`, so a later module reaching for held-out data still fails a test.
- **Older models with more than 50 entries: a backward-compatible rule with explicit tests (from the formal review of #166).** Importing, loading, replaying and exporting a model never truncates, rejects or rewrites its `evaluation` array; the only bound on import is the existing whole-document size limit. The cap of 50 applies **only when appending this app's own entries**, counts only those, and a model at or over it **refuses the append with a message and is otherwise untouched**. Tests: a pre-change fixture (written by the unchanged code) with **51** opaque entries and others with 120 and with 60 of this app's own kind: each imports, round-trips byte for byte through import and export, and an append to each is refused with the cap message and leaves the stored document byte-identical.
- **The page: `?mode=evaluate` (new page mode).** The default page, `?mode=train` and `?mode=edit` are unchanged. A model list (from the model store; a damaged or non-drivable model is shown with its reason and cannot be run), then circuit checkboxes in two labelled groups: **Other training circuits** and **Validation circuits**, with the circuit the model was trained on marked and not offered as "unfamiliar" (it may be ticked, and is labelled "training circuit"). **Run** evaluates the chosen circuits in short slices (`setTimeout` yields, so the page stays responsive on a phone), with a progress line and a **Cancel** that keeps the circuits already finished; each finished circuit is stored in the model's `evaluation` field through a new `appendEvaluation` store operation that validates the entry and writes it the way a save does (an all-or-nothing write, with the storage-full and disabled-storage messages the saved-champions panel already has). The results table shows, per circuit and split: lap completion, collision rate, outcome counts, mean first-lap time with n, mean ordered progress, and a note that a deterministic model on five starts is one model's behaviour, not a statistic. All controls are at least 44 px and keyboard operable; the table is a real `<table>` with a caption and the status line is a polite live region; nothing scrolls sideways at 320 px. Results are shown on this page only: they never reach the training dashboard or graph, the comparison view (which never ranks by evaluation results) or automatic champion selection.
- **Determinism and replay.** Evaluation is a pure function of the model, the circuit's recorded hash and the protocol, in one engine: running it twice gives identical entries (apart from `recordedAt`), and a test shows that **the stored entry replays**: re-running the protocol on the recorded model, circuit and seed list reproduces every row. Cross-browser differences are recorded, not hidden.
- **No change to the trainer, the split code or the formats beyond the `evaluation` entry kind.** The three-split boundaries and the study held-out gate are untouched, and no module is added to the source-scan allowlist for `heldOutTrack`.

## 4. What is deliberately not claimed

Evaluating one model on a few circuits from a few starts shows how that model drove there; it does not show that evolution generalises, that braking helps, or that any rule or baseline is beaten: those are studies (P5-04, E3, E5, E6) with preregistered protocols, ten training seeds and the command line. Perturbed starts are scenario variation, not independent trials. Lap time is simulation time. Validation and other training circuits are circuits a model did not train on, **not** held-out circuits: nothing here is an unseen-track generalisation claim. No learning claim is made.

## 5. Decisions for the owner (defaults proposed; each routine unless changed)

1. **No held-out circuit in the browser (decided: the owner's "Let's do it then", 2026-11-12, after the recommendation to drop it).** Held-out evaluation exists only through the preregistered command-line studies. The roadmap's P4-05 E2E line is changed to a validation track and another training track. Reasons: a model hash cannot stop exploratory results shaping a later study; the machinery to guard a held-out look (an exploratory circuit and registry, cross-parser overlap rules, a claim ledger, cross-tab locking, interrupted-look states) is the largest and riskiest part of the feature for the least benefit; an exploratory circuit would stop being "unseen" as soon as it was used; and P4-03's own circuits give users an unregistered layout nobody has looked at.
2. **Start protocol:** the nominal start plus four seeded perturbed starts (recommended, so five episodes per circuit show some sensitivity), or the nominal start only (one episode per circuit, simplest, no start variation).
3. **A new page mode `?mode=evaluate`** (recommended) rather than a panel inside the training page, which is already crowded on a phone.
4. **The entry format** `p4-05-evaluation-v1`, strictly validated for its own entries only, with unknown kinds kept opaque and **no new count or per-entry rule applied to existing documents** (a model with more than 50 entries still imports, loads and exports byte for byte); the model schema version is unchanged.
5. **Evaluation results never feed the dashboard, the comparison view or automatic selection**, and are never ranked.
6. **At most 50 of this app's own `p4-05-evaluation-v1` entries per model, enforced only when appending** (never on import), stored in the model's `evaluation` field in browser storage with the existing safety rules.
7. **`modelEvaluation.ts` on the source-scan allowlist for `trainingTrack` and `validationTrack` only**, resolving circuit **IDs** and never receiving a `Track` from the page; nothing is added for `heldOutTrack`.

## 6. Tests

- **Unit (`modelEvaluation`):** the protocol's constants; the start perturbations are deterministic for a seed, within the stated bounds and always on the road without a wall touch (checked on **every** built-in circuit and a sweep of seeds); the driver is built from the model's own brake flag (an A4-type model really has its brake masked); the summary equals a hand recomputation from the rows on fixed fixtures (lap completion, collision rate, outcome counts, mean first-lap time with n, `null` and `0` with no completed lap); timeouts and stalls stay in the denominators; hostile models (wrong parameter count, non-finite values, an unfrozen copy) are refused with messages, never a raw exception.
- **Integration (determinism and replay):** evaluating the same saved model twice gives identical entries but for `recordedAt`; a stored entry replays row for row; a tampered row, count, hash or seed list is refused by the validator; every older model file (including the committed fixtures) still imports and its empty `evaluation` is valid.
- **Held-out stays out (circuits, not hashes):** `modelEvaluation` refuses **every** held-out ID, a renamed copy of a held-out circuit and a copy under another registered ID, with `TrackLeakageError`, **including for a frozen, valid model**, and produces and displays no figure for one; a source scan fails if `modelEvaluation.ts`, `evaluatePage.ts` or any test names a held-out circuit ID or imports `heldOutTrack` or `buildEvaluationSetup`, and the allowlist names only `modelEvaluation.ts` (for `trainingTrack` and `validationTrack`); the page's groups come from `tracksIn('training')` and `tracksIn('validation')` only; no evaluation figure reaches the dashboard, the saved list or the comparison view (a source scan and a rendering check); **CI never evaluates a model on a held-out circuit**.
- **Store and compatibility:** `appendEvaluation` is all-or-nothing, counts only this app's own entries toward the cap of 50 **and only when appending**, respects the size limit, reports storage-full and disabled-storage and leaves other models untouched; **a model that already has 120 opaque entries, and one with 60 entries of this app's kind, still imports, loads, replays and exports byte for byte** (the committed fixtures plus new ones written by the unchanged code before the format change); an old model that is over the cap cannot take another entry and says why, and is otherwise untouched.
- **Page wiring (Node, fakes) and E2E (Chromium and WebKit, desktop, phone and tablet):** save a champion, open `?mode=evaluate`, evaluate it on the **other training circuit** and a **validation circuit** (the roadmap's E2E as amended), see the table, reload and see the stored results; cancel keeps the finished circuits; a storage failure shows the message and stores nothing; a damaged model cannot be run; the page offers no held-out circuit; 44 px controls and no horizontal scroll at 320 px.
- **Mutation checks** during implementation: weakening the one-look rule, the frozen-model requirement, the summary denominators, the start bounds and the entry validation each fails a test.
- Not tested here: a physical device, and any statistical claim.

## 7. Owner checks (iPhone and iPad, before a completion request)

1. Save a champion in `?mode=train`, open `?mode=evaluate`, pick it, tick one training and one validation circuit and run: the progress line moves, the table appears, nothing scrolls sideways and the page stays responsive in portrait and landscape.
2. Reload: the results are still there, and the page lists no held-out circuit; the controls are easy to tap (44 px).

## 8. Limits

One saved model, a handful of circuits and five starts: behaviour, not evidence. Results can differ between browsers and engines. P4-03 adds saved custom circuits as targets of the same protocol; P4-07 adds camera work that this page does not need. ST1 to ST5, the held-out gate and ledger used by the studies, every existing record and the E3 protocol are untouched.
