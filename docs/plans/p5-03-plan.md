## 1. What P5-03 must deliver (ROADMAP, issue #35)

- **Description:** race a human-driven car against a loaded model on the same track (simultaneous non-colliding cars), with lap time comparison.
- **Acceptance:** human and AI use the same physics and start; the results screen shows both lap times, completion or failure and independent wall collisions; playable on iPhone.
- **Tests:** integration: the AI car is deterministic during races. E2E: start and finish a race with touch controls.

## 3. Design

- **`src/training/race.ts` (new, pure, headless).** `createRace(setup)` and `stepRace(state, humanControls)` advance **two independent racers** one fixed tick at a time on one track: the human's, driven by the controls passed in, and the AI's, driven by `createNetworkDriver` through the same `stepEpisode` the trainer and the evaluation use (so the AI path is the trainer's path). Each racer is built with `startRacer` at the track's start pose, with the same physics and timestep, and has its **own** collision, crash flag and checkpoint progress. **The cars never interact**: no car-to-car collision, and the AI's ray sensors read the track's walls only, so the human car is invisible to the AI and to the physics. A racer's race ends when it completes the chosen number of laps (`completed`), touches a wall (`crashed`, after which it earns nothing more, as everywhere in the simulation), or the race clock runs out (`timeout`: **60 s per chosen lap, this plan's proposal**, recorded in the result; the trainer's own 60 s covers a whole three-lap episode, which is too short for a person on a long circuit, and a longer clock does not change the deterministic AI's path); the AI's stall rule is the trainer's (5 s without a new gate credit), applied to **both** cars so the same rules judge both. The race ends when both racers have ended.
- **Same physics and the same start (the acceptance criterion).** Both cars use **the model's own recorded physics and timestep** (so the AI drives under exactly the conditions it was saved with, and the human drives the same car), from the **same start pose and tick 0**. The human keeps the brake control (a person's driving input); if the champion's brake was masked (a no-brake model), the results screen says "the AI had no brake" beside the comparison. A model this build cannot drive (a compatibility refusal) is listed with its reason and cannot be raced, as for replay.
- **Determinism.** The AI side reads only the genome and the setup, so its trajectory in a race is **identical to its solo run on that circuit** whatever the human does, and identical on every rerun. A test steps a race with several scripted human inputs (idle, full throttle into a wall, a scripted lap) and shows the AI's state at every tick equals the AI-only `stepEpisode` run, tick by tick, and that two races with the same human inputs are identical. The camera, the countdown and any render time never enter `race.ts`.
- **The results (`raceResult(state)`).** For each racer: the outcome (`completed`, `crashed`, `timeout`, `stalled`), laps completed, the **first-lap time and the best lap in simulated seconds** (ticks times the timestep, `null` and "no lap completed" when none), the **total time for the chosen laps** when completed, gates credited (ordered progress), the tick of the wall collision if any, and the **number of wall collisions** (a car's run ends at its first wall contact in this simulation, so this is 0 or 1 per run; the results say so). A comparison line appears only when it is true: "You were faster over N laps by X s" only when both completed; "the AI crashed at gate k" or "You crashed" otherwise; no "winner" is declared when either car did not complete. Nothing is stored (decision 6).
- **Choosing the race (`ui/raceController.ts`, DOM-free).** A saved champion from the model store (usable ones only), a circuit (default: the circuit the champion was trained on; also the other training circuits, the validation circuits and the person's saved custom tracks through the P4-03 source, **never a held-out circuit**, resolved only through `trainingTrack`, `validationTrack` and the custom source), and a race length of 1 or 3 laps (default 3). **Start** begins a three-second countdown on render time (the simulation does not run during it), then the race runs on the page's fixed-step loop.
- **The page: `?mode=race` (new page mode).** The other pages are unchanged. One canvas drawn through the P4-07 camera (default: whole track; Follow follows **your** car, with a toggle to follow the AI), your car and the AI's in **two fixed identity colours with the labels "You" and "AI"** (colour is never the only cue), a HUD with both cars' laps and times, the existing on-screen Left, Right, Gas and Brake controls and the keyboard, a Restart that starts the whole race again from the same start (the AI repeats exactly), and a results panel when both have ended. All controls at least 44 px, the page does not scroll under the canvas, and nothing here is specific to a phone except that it must work on one.
- **No change to the simulation, the trainer, the model format or the studies.** `race.ts` imports the simulation and the driver types only; nothing in `sim`, `ai`, `evolution`, `training/trainer`, `persistence` or the held-out gate imports it.

## 4. What is deliberately not claimed

One race between one person and one deterministic champion on one circuit shows how that race went, not who drives better in general: a person varies from run to run, the champion does not, and a champion trained on a circuit has seen it. Lap times are simulated time. No learning or generalisation claim is made; a loss or a win says nothing about evolution. Cars do not touch each other, so this is not a test of overtaking or defence (R-01). A race on a validation circuit is not a held-out evaluation.

## 5. Decisions for the owner (defaults proposed; each routine unless changed)

1. **A fifth page mode `?mode=race`;** the default page and the other modes are unchanged.
2. **Both cars use the model's own recorded physics and timestep, from one start pose;** the human keeps the brake, and the results say when the AI had none.
3. **Simultaneous, non-colliding cars:** no car-to-car contact and no effect of one car on the other's sensors (physical competition stays R-01).
4. **Race length 1 or 3 laps (default 3);** a car's race ends on completion, a wall contact, the clock (60 s per chosen lap, a proposal of this plan) or the trainer's 5 s stall rule (no new gate credit for 5 s), applied to both.
5. **Circuits:** the champion's training circuit by default, the other training and validation circuits, and saved custom tracks; never a held-out circuit.
6. **Race results are not stored** (not in the model's `evaluation`, not in browser storage); each race is shown on its own results panel.
7. **A three-second countdown on render time** before the simulation runs; Restart repeats the same race.
8. **The P4-07 camera is reused:** whole track by default, Follow follows your car with a toggle for the AI.

## 6. Tests

- **Unit (`race`):** the rules from hand fixtures: each racer's outcome (completed, crashed, timeout, stalled), laps and gate credits counted separately, the race ends only when both have ended, a crashed car earns nothing more and its tick is recorded; lap times equal ticks times the timestep (checked against the existing `lapSeconds`); a comparison line is produced only when both completed and is otherwise the right failure sentence; the first-lap and best-lap and total time with and without completed laps (`null`, not zero); hostile inputs (non-finite controls, a missing model parameter count) give messages and never throw.
- **Integration (determinism and independence):** the AI's state at **every tick** of a race equals the AI-only `stepEpisode` run on the same circuit and start, for several scripted human inputs (idle, full throttle into a wall, a scripted lap, random seeded inputs), and two races with the same inputs are identical; the human car's position never changes what the AI senses or does (a test moves the human car through the AI's rays and shows the AI unchanged); both cars start at tick 0 from the same pose with the same physics object values; the camera, countdown and drawing leave a race's state bit-identical (as for P4-07). A mutation (letting the human car block the AI's sensors, or giving the AI a different timestep) fails these tests.
- **Page wiring (Node, fakes):** the chooser lists usable champions and the allowed circuits only and never offers a held-out ID (the P4-05 source-scan pattern: no held-out circuit named in the race code); a damaged or non-drivable model cannot be started; Start runs the countdown without stepping the simulation, then races; Restart repeats the same AI trajectory; the results panel follows the true outcomes.
- **E2E (Chromium and WebKit, desktop, phone and tablet):** with a saved champion in browser storage, open `?mode=race`, choose a champion and a 1-lap race, **start and finish a race with the on-screen touch controls** (the touch projects drive with taps and holds; a human crash or a clock end is a valid finish), see both cars drawn with their labels, see the results panel with both outcomes and the correct comparison sentence, Restart and see the AI repeat; no held-out circuit offered; 44 px controls and no sideways scroll at 320 px; the other pages show no race UI.
- Not tested here: a physical device, and any statement about who drives better.

## 7. Owner checks (iPhone, before a completion request)

1. Save a champion in `?mode=train`, open `?mode=race`, choose it and a 1-lap race, and drive with the on-screen Left, Right, Gas and Brake controls: both cars are visible and labelled, the controls respond, and the page does not scroll or zoom under your thumbs.
2. Finish (or crash out of) the race: the results panel is readable in portrait and landscape and states both outcomes truthfully; Restart repeats the AI's run.

## 8. Limits

Two cars that never touch, one saved champion at a time, results shown and not stored, circuits limited to training, validation and your saved tracks. A person's single race is not evidence about the AI. ST1 to ST4, the three-split boundaries, the study held-out gate and ledger, every existing record and the E3 protocol are untouched.
