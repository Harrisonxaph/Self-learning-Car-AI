## 3. Design (defaults proposed; each is a decision for the owner and Codex, section 7)

**3.1 The question and the measure.** The claim is about **lap completion** at a rate **clearly above baselines**. So the deciding endpoint is the **lap-completion rate** per agent class and circuit (the share of its episodes, over the five fixed starts, in which a lap was completed within the episode limit), compared with each baseline's by a predeclared meaningful difference (proposed **0.20**, one start in five, as E5's collision margin). **Ordered progress** (laps, the E3 and E4 measure) is kept as an additional **descriptive** endpoint and decides nothing. Nothing here says anything about braking (that is E5); A4 and A5 are both "evolved" classes in E6.

**3.2 The agents** (each frozen before the look, by content identity):
- **A5 and A4:** the ten E4 champions of each (seeds 2001-2010). **Under the shared-circuit option of decision 1 (the default)**, their held-out evaluation is E5's (the gate allows each (model, circuit) once): E6 **reads it from E5's record**, checked against E5's manifest hash, and does not evaluate them again; E6 then cannot be analysed until E5 has been opened. **Under the second-batch option**, E5's record holds nothing for the new circuits (its manifest pins only `hairpin-run`, `sweep-hook` and `hook-chicane`), so E6 needs its **own, separately authorised A4/A5 evaluation** of the same ten champions on the new circuits, recorded in E6's held-out record through the gate (a new (model, circuit) pair each, so nothing of E5 is reopened or changed); E6 is then independent of E5's opening and may be analysed without it.
- **A3:** the frozen rule (one deterministic value per circuit, as in E3). A3-nb and A3-nb-same stay E3's.
- **A2 (untrained network):** per seed, **one** frozen representative of the seed's generation 0, chosen by a **predeclared rule that uses no performance** (proposed: the **first genome** of that seed's generation-0 population, brake active, as every A5-style network), so it is "an untrained network" and not the best of a population (that is A9, outside E6).
- **A1 (random action) and A7 (correlated random action):** random drivers have no model; their identity is the driver kind, the action-stream derivation (`actionSeed`) and a **predeclared set of action seeds** (proposed 10, matching the ten training seeds). They need a frozen identity in the gate (section 4, step 2).

**3.3 The units, the comparisons and the inference contract.** There are three kinds of cohort, and the plan fixes how each comparison relates them (pairing is **never inferred from array order or from matching numeric labels**):
- **Evolved seeds (A5 or A4):** the ten E4 training seeds are the units.
- **A2 (untrained network):** paired with the evolved class **by training seed**, because the A2 representative is the first genome of that same seed's generation 0 (the same initialisation stream); this is the one **paired** comparison, resampled as one cluster per seed (the same seed index drawn for both).
- **A1 and A7 (random drivers):** the action seeds are **independently sampled units** with no relation to the training seeds, so these comparisons use **independent resampling of the two cohorts** (each cohort resampled on its own, with replacement). Position or numeric closeness of the seeds defines no pairing (an analytical check: the same two marginal cohorts give different paired intervals by reordering one of them, so a pairing must be defined by design, not by index).
- **A3:** deterministic, **one fixed five-start summary per circuit**, treated as a constant with no sampling variation (as in E3); only the evolved cohort is resampled.

Per circuit, per evolved class (A5, A4) against each baseline (A1, A7, A2, A3): the mean difference in lap-completion rate with a **percentile bootstrap** using the existing constants (10,000 resamples, 95%, percentile with nearest rank, as `E5_BOOTSTRAP`) and **one new predeclared bootstrap seed, 20261115**, with an independent deterministic stream per cohort (fixed in the preregistration); labels `evolved-higher`, `baseline-higher`, `no-clear-difference` by the E3 and E5 rule (interval entirely beyond zero and the mean at least the margin). **Secondary, descriptive summaries kept as AI_EVALUATION section 8 requires:** the **paired Wilcoxon signed-rank test** for the A2 comparison and the **unpaired Mann-Whitney U test** for the A1 and A7 comparisons (the existing `WILCOXON` settings in `study.ts`; the Mann-Whitney U test is a new, analytically tested function in step 3), always with n; A3's single value has no test (the report shows the evolved cohort's distribution beside it). They decide nothing: the label comes from the bootstrap rule only, and a change of that criterion would need an owner-approved amendment, not silent omission.

**Multiplicity.** There are 24 contrasts (2 evolved classes × 4 baselines × 3 circuits). They are reported with **pointwise 95% intervals and no multiplicity adjustment**, stated in the report, so each label is a pointwise statement and some `evolved-higher` labels would be expected by chance across 24 contrasts even if nothing were real. A **circuit-level generalisation statement** for an evolved class on a circuit is made **only if all four of its baseline contrasts on that circuit are `evolved-higher`** (an intersection of the four, so it is at least as strict as each single contrast, and the 24 are never combined into a pooled count). **No pooled headline across circuits and no pooled "evolved beats baselines" claim**; A4 and A5 are reported separately; if the circuits disagree the report says so and stops.

**3.4 A floor is a descriptive annotation, never a veto on the endpoint.** The deciding endpoint (section 3.3) decides on its own: its label stands whatever the floor annotation says. Where most agents of every class complete no lap on a circuit, the report **adds** a floor annotation with the actual completion and progress distributions shown (never as the likeliest explanation of a weak result, and never prevented by editing a circuit). A floor does not suppress a supported difference: for example, four evolved seeds completing every start against baselines completing none can give `evolved-higher` on the endpoint **and** a floor note (most evolved seeds completed nothing), and the report says both and says the supported difference rests on a minority of seeds. Where the endpoint is `no-clear-difference` and the floor holds, the report says the circuit was too hard for every class to separate them, as an untested possibility. Completion rates near zero for all classes are a valid finding.

**3.5 What E6 can and cannot say.** It can say, per circuit and for **these** champions, whether evolved drivers completed laps on a circuit they never trained on at a clearly higher rate than these baselines. It cannot say anything about other training configurations, seeds or circuits, about braking (E5), or about "learning" in general; "no clear difference" does not mean equivalence.

## 4. Steps (each needs its own owner authorisation; this plan authorises none)

1. **This plan** (documentation only).
2. **Gate extension:** frozen identities for the random drivers (A1, A7) and for an untrained-network A2 representative, with the E3 and E5 safeguards unchanged (supported names and bindings validated before any driver is built, each (model, circuit) once, the ordinary guards still refuse held-out circuits). Negative tests; no change to existing identities.
3. **E6 library:** the manifest (frozen commit, the E5 manifest hash where E5's record is reused, the agents' identities and seeds, the circuits, the margin and bootstrap, the decision rule), the held-out record (**shared-circuit option: baselines only, with A4 and A5 read from E5's record and checked against its hash; second-batch option: the baselines and the A4 and A5 champions, all evaluated through the gate**), strict parsers, replay, the analysis and the report; synthetic fixtures and analytical tests, including a floor annotation alongside a supported endpoint, and a floor with no clear difference.

6. **The run (a held-out opening).** A one-way action: it needs the owner's explicit go-ahead naming the `manifestHash`, in the recorded Node 24.22.1 environment, once, through the gate and a ledger outside the worktree.
7. **Results PR and the report:** records, ledger and analysis unchanged, the E6 entry and the generalisation results page in `docs/AI_EVALUATION.md` with limitations, then completion (the owner's).

## 6. What is deliberately not claimed

- E6 is **not** a test of braking and does not revise E5, E3 or Phase 2; its results hold for these ten champions per class, this configuration (C1), these three circuits and these baselines.
- The fresh circuits are exposed by the E5 opening. Using them for E6 means **E5's results are known when E6 runs**; the protection is that **E6's design is frozen before E5 opens** (decision 1). If the owner prefers independence, decision 1's alternative buys it at the price of another circuit batch.
- The designer saw E3's results and the circuit geometry (disclosed already in the circuits plan); that stays true for any circuit batch.
- A lap-completion rate over five fixed starts is scenario variation of a deterministic agent, **not independent samples**; intervals are over the ten seeds only.

## 7. Decisions for the owner (defaults proposed; each routine unless changed)

1. **Which held-out circuits for E6.** *Default (recommended):* **the same three fresh circuits as E5**, with E6's design **preregistered before E5 opens**, so E5's result cannot shape E6; A4 and A5 are read from E5's record and only the baselines are newly evaluated. *Alternative:* a **second batch of fresh circuits** (a repeat of the circuits plan: specs, review, generation, epoch 5), which makes E6 independent of E5 but adds roughly three more reviewed pull requests **and a separately authorised A4/A5 evaluation of the ten champions on the new circuits** (E5's record cannot supply them). *Not recommended:* designing E6 after E5's result is known and reusing the circuits.
2. **Sequencing with E5.** *Default:* complete steps 2 to 5 (E6's preregistration) **before** the E5 opening is requested, so E5 and E6 are opened one after the other under frozen designs. *Alternative:* open E5 first (today's plan) and accept the weaker protection of decision 1's alternative, or an E6 on fresh circuits.
3. **The deciding measure:** lap-completion rate, with ordered progress descriptive (default), against progress as the decider (the superseded P5-02 rule, which failed review).
4. **The margin, the agent definitions and the inference contract:** 0.20 (default), the A2 representative rule (the first genome of generation 0), 10 random-driver action seeds, and A3 as the only rule agent (A3-nb and A3-nb-same stay E3's); **and the inference contract of section 3.3** (paired resampling for A2 by training seed, independent resampling for A1 and A7, A3 as a constant, the new bootstrap seed 20261115, the Wilcoxon and Mann-Whitney summaries kept as descriptive, pointwise intervals with no multiplicity adjustment and the all-four-contrasts rule for a circuit-level statement).
5. **Is P5-04 to be done at all before the owner's other priorities** (P5-03's completion, P5-06): this plan is documentation only and delays nothing else.

## 8. Tests (for the later steps)

Identity tests for the new agents (the random drivers' action streams, the untrained representative, refusal of an unsupported name or binding); gate tests (once per (model, circuit), no listed-agent bypass); analysis against hand-computed cases (the paired and the independent bootstrap on the same marginals giving different intervals, the Mann-Whitney U and Wilcoxon summaries against hand calculations, a floor annotation that does not veto a supported endpoint, a floor with no clear difference, all agents completing, one baseline above the evolved class, ties, A3's single value); record round trip, corruption refusals and replay equality; the claims text (no pooled headline, no equivalence from a null). No test evaluates a model on a real held-out circuit.
